## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** This is the rare case where the answer is a clear no.

**LLM assessment.** The reflex answers were all avoided and the file that documents why reads like a person made the calls. Navy was rejected for an accounting site. Playfair and Cormorant were rejected, and so were Inter, IBM Plex, and Newsreader, which the author names as their own reflex picks. The violet is pulled from a real object in the photograph and driven dark enough to function as ink. Corners are square sitewide, cards are refused in favor of ruled rows, and the `mix-blend-mode: multiply` portrait compositing against paper so the hairlines read through the high-key background is a genuine mechanic, not decoration. Verified in the browser: it works.

Two reservations.

The site sits closer to the **editorial-typographic lane** than its own anti-references admit. The brand register names that lane as currently saturated: display serif, small tracked labels, hairline rules, monochromatic restraint. What shipped is a display serif, uppercase tracked Public Sans labels used as recurring section grammar, hairline separators, and violet-on-paper restraint. The ledger framing and the committed violet pull it clear of a clone, but "the second-order reflex" applies. Someone given the brief plus the anti-reference list ("faculty page, but not the stock one, and not magazine either") could plausibly arrive here.

The brand ban on "repeated tiny uppercase tracked labels above every section heading" is technically cleared because DESIGN.md names it as a deliberate system. It clears on the documentation, not on the delivery. A visitor never reads DESIGN.md, and what they see is uppercase tracked labels in the nav, the buttons, the gutter numerals, the strip, the fact lists, the episode numbers, and the press outlets. The system is asserted more strongly in the spec than it is felt in the pixels.

**Deterministic scan: unavailable.** `detect.mjs` exits with "bundled detector not found." The detector bundle is absent at every fallback path (`scripts/detector/`, `../../cli/engine/`, `node_modules/impeccable/`), for both the CLI and browser variants. The overlay path is therefore also unavailable, so no user-visible overlay was injected and none should be expected in the browser.

**Fallback: manual pattern sweep** across `src/`, targeting the same bans:

| Pattern | Result |
|---|---|
| Side-stripe borders (`border-left/right` accent) | none |
| Gradient text (`background-clip: text`) | none |
| Glassmorphism (`backdrop-filter`, `blur()`) | none |
| Decorative gradients | none (2 hits are `mask-image` alpha ramps, false positives) |
| `#000` / `#fff` | 2 hits, both mask channels in `Home.css:110,196`, false positives |

Contrast audit, measured in-page against the DESIGN.md table: every documented ratio is accurate to within 0.03. Body ink 13.72:1, muted 6.46:1, violet 7.20:1, nav links on the masthead band 9.41:1. That table was not guessed.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-made object with a shipping problem and a follow-through problem.

The hero is the best thing here and it earns its place: the portrait composites into the paper instead of sitting on it, the rules draw through her, and the whole thing reads as printed rather than assembled. Then the concept stops. Four of the five pages carry the ledger as a single 1px line at 1.39:1 contrast and a two-digit numeral floating in an otherwise empty 110px gutter, with the right 40% of the page unclaimed. The metaphor that justifies every other decision is present on one screen and gestured at on the rest.

The single biggest opportunity: **finish the ledger on the interior pages.** The publication and episode lists are exactly where a worksheet's ruled columns belong, and they are currently the emptiest surfaces on the site.

## What's Working

**The portrait mechanic.** `mix-blend-mode: multiply` against an explicit paper ground with `isolation: isolate`, a directional mask, and the rules layer sitting beneath. The comment at `Home.css:107` warning that a `filter` would cancel the blend in Chrome is the kind of note you only write after hitting it. This is the site's identity and it survives contact with a real browser.

**The token discipline.** `tokens.css` and DESIGN.md agree to the decimal, and the accessibility claims are measurably true rather than aspirational. `--rule` and `--rule-strong` are documented as decorative-only with `--rule-ui` reserved for anything interactive, and the code honors it. Most projects that write this table do not then obey it.

**The placeholder system.** Every unfilled string renders with a violet highlight via `Text.tsx`, centralized in one file, with the highlight disappearing on its own when real copy lands. The Podcast cover art is a correctly-proportioned marked reservation rather than a decorative gradient block pretending to be artwork. Refusing to invent facts about a real person, and making that refusal visible, is the right call.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Deep links and refreshes 404 in production

**What.** `RouterProvider` reads `window.location.pathname` and the build is a plain Vite SPA. There is no `public/_redirects`, no `netlify.toml`, no host rewrite rule. `dist/index.html` ships `<div id="root"></div>` and nothing else. It works in `vite dev` only because the dev server has history fallback built in.

**Why it matters.** Every arrival path PRODUCT.md describes is a deep link. A coauthor pastes `/research` into Slack. A journalist bookmarks `/podcast`. A dean opens a link between meetings and hits refresh. All of them get the host's unstyled 404. This is the one bug on the site that stops the primary task cold, and it is invisible in local development.

**Fix.** Add `public/_redirects` containing `/*  /index.html  200`, or a `netlify.toml` with the equivalent rewrite. Then reconsider whether a five-page content site needs client-side routing at all: prerendering to five static HTML files removes the 201K JS boot, gives crawlers and link unfurls real content, and eliminates this class of bug permanently.

**Suggested command:** `harden`

### [P1] Home never fits the one viewport it is built around

**What.** DESIGN.md: "Home is exactly one viewport. It does not scroll on desktop." Measured, it scrolls at every size tested:

| Viewport | Document height | Overflow |
|---|---|---|
| 1440 × 723 | 860 | 137px |
| 1366 × 768 | 862 | 94px |
| 1180 × 760 | 849 | 89px |
| 1024 × 700 | 800 | 100px |
| 768 × 1024 | 1106 | 82px |
| 390 × 780 | 1206 | 426px |

**Why it matters.** The ledger strip is the element that makes Home read as a ruled sheet rather than a hero section, and it is guillotined everywhere. Worse, it overflows by 82 to 137 pixels: the exact amount that reads as a mistake rather than an invitation. It also holds the institutional facts (Institution, School, Field, Standing), which is the only content aimed at the dean and donor audience PRODUCT.md says the hero page carries. That audience currently sees nothing addressed to them. And the strip's entrance animation, delayed 720ms, plays where no one can see it.

On mobile the failure is larger: at 390 × 780 the portrait sits at y=695, so the single most important visual mechanic on the site is entirely below the fold on a phone.

**Fix.** Pick one and commit. Either make the promise true (cut `--band` on the hero at short viewports, reduce `--fs-display`'s upper reach, tighten the CTA stack) or abandon it and design a deliberate scroll where the strip is a real second beat with its own top rule. The current state is neither.

**Suggested command:** `adapt`

### [P1] The ledger concept does not survive past the hero

**What.** Home has three vertical rules, a numbered gutter, a portrait printed into the ruling, and a four-cell strip. `/about`, `/research`, `/podcast`, and `/contact` have one 1px line at `--rule` (1.39:1 against paper, close to imperceptible) and a numeral in a 110px gutter that is otherwise empty. Row separators use the same 1.39:1 hairline. The right 40% of every interior page is unclaimed white.

**Why it matters.** DESIGN.md says structure is the ornament and there is no other ornament. On four of five pages the ornament is below the threshold of perception, which leaves those pages with no visual identity at all beyond the typeface and the masthead band. It is also self-defeating: the design refused cards on the grounds that ruled rows are the truer document form, then specified the rules too faintly to do the job cards were doing.

**Fix.** Promote row separators and the gutter rule to `--rule-strong` (2.10:1) and let `--rule` handle only the finest interior ruling. Then claim the right column: run a vertical rule down the boundary between the entry body and the link cluster so the ledger's ruled-column logic is visible, and give the empty right band something a worksheet would put there (year, status, citation count, outlet).

**Suggested command:** `layout`

### [P1] Route changes are silent to screen readers and to focus

**What.** `navigate()` pushes state, sets `path`, and scrolls to top. `document.title` updates in an effect. Nothing moves focus and nothing announces the change. Focus stays on the nav link that was activated while the entire `<main>` swaps beneath it.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md commits to WCAG 2.2 AA and the rest of the accessibility work is real: skip link, landmarks, one `h1` per page, `aria-current` alongside a visible rule so nothing depends on color, focus rings never removed, verified contrast. This is the one hole, and it is the standard SPA hole. A screen reader user activating "Research" hears nothing and has no idea the page changed.

**Fix.** On path change (skipping first render), move focus to the `<main>` element, which is already `tabIndex={-1}` and ready for it, and add a visually hidden `aria-live="polite"` region announcing the new page title.

**Suggested command:** `harden`

### [P2] Publication rows break the proximity they depend on

**What.** `.entry` is `minmax(0, 1fr) minmax(0, 16rem)` with the link cluster right-aligned. At 1440 the title ends around x=450 and "Abstract / PDF / SSRN" begins around x=1310. Roughly 850px of empty paper separates a title from its own links, bound only by a 1.39:1 hairline. The same gap sits between an episode title and its play button.

**Why it matters.** Design Principle 5 is "Findable beats impressive," and the reason given for choosing rows over cards was faster scanning for a peer hunting a PDF. In practice a card kept the title and its links inside one bounded object; the row now asks the eye to travel the full page width and trust a nearly invisible line to tell it which row it landed on. The gestalt that rows depend on, proximity, is the weakest thing in the layout. This is also the one cognitive-load checklist item the site fails.

**Fix.** Cap the entry grid at a real measure rather than letting `1fr` eat the viewport (`minmax(0, 46rem)` for the body), which pulls the link cluster back into scanning range on wide screens. Add a vertical rule at the column boundary so the association is drawn, not inferred. Consider linking the title itself, which is what everyone tries first.

**Suggested command:** `layout`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User).** At 390 × 780 the masthead eats 110px (14% of the screen) on a two-row stack that repeats on every page. The portrait does not appear until y=695, so the site's signature image is invisible on first paint. The role line renders as three stacked violet placeholder blocks that look like a rendering error before real copy lands. Three CTAs stack vertically and push everything else down. The page is 1206px against a 780px viewport, so nothing about the "one viewport" concept survives on the device where most podcast listeners will arrive.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent).** Most of the walk is clean: tab order is skip link, wordmark, five nav items, `main`, content; focus rings are visible on both paper and the violet band via the `.on-violet` override; contrast passes AA everywhere with room to spare; the decorative rules and "/" separators are correctly `aria-hidden`. Then Sam activates "Research" and nothing happens audibly. Focus stays on the link, no announcement fires, and the only clue is the title change, which most screen readers do not read on a same-document update. Sam then refreshes to recover and, in production, lands on a host 404.

**The peer hunting a PDF (from PRODUCT.md, audience 1).** Arrives at `/research` from a link, which in production is already a 404. Past that, the list works: links are always visible, never hover-gated, exactly as promised. But there is no search, no filter by year or journal, no anchor to jump to Working Papers, and no citation or BibTeX affordance. With three placeholder rows this is fine. With forty real publications, the peer will use Cmd-F and the ruled-row scan the design was built around stops paying off.

**The dean or donor (from PRODUCT.md, audience 3).** PRODUCT.md says the hero page carries the whole weight for this reader and must signal seriousness within three seconds. In practice: the page arrives blank while 201K of JavaScript parses, the fonts swap and the 82px headline reflows, and the ledger strip carrying every institutional fact is cut off below the fold. If the link came through Slack or email, the unfurl shows no image and a generic description, because there is no `og:image` and no per-route metadata. The three seconds are spent on a blank screen and a reflow.

## Minor Observations

- **Two em dashes in shipped copy.** `App.tsx:45,48` build `document.title` as `"Research & Publications — Bridget Stomberg, PhD"`. Browser tab text is copy. Use a middot or a pipe.
- **The footer repeats the masthead verbatim.** Same five links, same treatment, plus the wordmark again. For the reader whose entire judgment is formed by care, the last thing on the page is a copy-paste of the first. Put the things a departing visitor actually wants there: email, CV, SSRN profile, podcast subscribe.
- **Episode durations do not align on the digit.** DESIGN.md specifies a right-aligned tabular duration column. The implementation nests the duration inside a centered `min-width: 7.5rem` button, so `tabular-nums` has nothing to align against.
- **The play glyph navigates away.** `▶ 00:00` is an `<a href>` to an external episode page with no external-link signal. The triangle promises inline playback.
- **Three row types, three link models.** Publications: dead title plus a link cluster. Episodes: dead title plus a button. Press: the title itself is the link. Pick one grammar.
- **The hero choreography replays on every return to Home.** Verified: `hero-rise` restarts at `currentTime: 0` after Research to Home. Brand permits ambitious first-load motion; the fourth replay in a session is not first load. Gate it on a session flag.
- **The gutter rule ends in mid-air.** `.page::before` stops at the end of `<article>`, leaving a `--band` of unruled paper before the footer. On a site whose premise is a rule system, a rule terminating in nothing is a visible loose end.
- **`ledgerStrip` cell 4 contradicts Design Principle 3.** "Standing: [INSERT ranking or honor]" is rank announced, in a labeled cell, on the home page. Principle 3 says rank is shown through the density of real work, never through a badge. The slot is built to be filled with the thing the principle forbids.
- **`index.html` asserts facts that `content.ts` refuses to invent.** The meta description names "Kelley School of Business, Indiana University" while every corresponding string in `content.ts` is `[INSERT university]`. One of the two files is wrong on principle.
- **The in-app 404 returns HTTP 200** and points at "the navigation above" rather than offering inline links to Research and Home.

## Questions to Consider

- The hero says "Bridging Academic Rigor and Tax Policy." Design Principle 2 says the typography should enact that bridge so the page does not have to claim it. If the two typefaces already argue the thesis, what could the largest words on the site say instead? A finding, a question, a plain declarative in her voice?
- The interior pages have an empty right 40%. What would a real worksheet put there?
- If the ledger only truly exists on Home, is it a site concept or a hero treatment? Which answer do you want to be true?
- What does this look like with forty publications instead of three? Does the ruled row still beat the card at that density, and what carries the scan?
- The footer is the last thing a dean sees. What should it say that the header does not?
